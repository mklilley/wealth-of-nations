    BEHIND THE BUBBLES

    A world of data.
    A little context.

    This visualization uses the three supplied historical datasets: income per person, life expectancy, and total population. They contain 195 countries and territories, with values from 1810 to 2020. All three files omit 1847; that year is explicitly shown as unavailable.

    How to read the chart

    Moving right means higher GDP per capita; moving up means longer life expectancy. The income axis is logarithmic: an equal distance represents an equal ratio. A bubble’s area is proportional to population, with a consistent scale across all years.

    Sources and geography

    The original CSVs are preserved in the repository and available below. Income is labeled as purchasing-power-parity (PPP), inflation adjusted. The files do not identify a dollar base year, so none is assumed. Historical values may be estimates.

    Continent assignments come from DataHub’s country codes, using its continent field and explicit aliases for country names. Transcontinental countries follow that dataset’s assignments.

    Missing values and animation

    A country appears only when all three measures exist for the selected year. Missing values are never replaced with zero. The year 1847 is absent from every source file; the chart leaves this gap empty. Movement between annual observations uses linear interpolation; pausing snaps to an actual annual observation. The summary includes only the visible countries, and average life expectancy is weighted by their populations.
